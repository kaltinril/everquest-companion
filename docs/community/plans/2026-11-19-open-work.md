## What the session taught about running one

- Land work in small pieces and answer the owner between them. Eight hours of stacked background
  jobs left the owner unable to get an answer or a testable build (owner, that evening: "8 hours +
  running is ridiculous, and you not being able to respond to me is annoying").
- Background jobs die or lose their tracking when the session restarts: a build that was half way
  left uncommitted edits, and a test run kept going with nobody listening. Commit often inside a
  long job, and never count on a job outliving the session.
- Heavy jobs side by side spoil each other's evidence: load experiments and screen probes made
  two e2e specs fail on a busy machine, and the engine specs count other test engines.
